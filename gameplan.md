# THE CRIMSON MUSEUM
### A Gothic First-Person Vampire-Hunting Action Game

**Engine:** Unity  
**Genre:** First-Person Action / Gothic Horror  
**Setting:** A sprawling Victorian mansion converted into a supernatural museum  
**Target:** Small-to-medium solo/indie project  
**Primary Inspiration:** Stylish action games, classic gothic horror, vampire fiction, and exploration-focused dungeon design

---

# 1. HIGH CONCEPT

*The Crimson Museum* is a fast-paced first-person action game in which the player takes the role of a vampire hunter investigating an enormous gothic mansion that has been transformed into a private museum.

The museum appears to be a collection dedicated to the history of vampires, monster hunting, and the occult.

However, the exhibits aren't merely historical artifacts.

Some are still alive.

The deeper the hunter travels into the mansion, the more the museum reveals its true purpose: it is part museum, part prison, and part ritual site belonging to an ancient vampire bloodline.

The game combines:

- Fast first-person combat
- Gunplay and melee combat
- Stylish movement and dodging
- Gothic exploration
- Environmental puzzles
- Vampire and monster encounters
- Large, theatrical boss battles
- A mysterious mansion that gradually changes as the player progresses

The intended experience is **action-first gothic horror**, rather than slow survival horror.

---

# 2. CORE DESIGN PILLARS

## Pillar 1 — Stylish Combat

The player should feel powerful and agile.

Combat should reward:

- Aggression
- Weapon switching
- Dodging
- Accuracy
- Movement
- Combining melee and ranged attacks
- Learning enemy behavior

The goal is not simply to survive encounters.

The goal is to **look good while surviving them.**

---

## Pillar 2 — The Mansion Is the World

The mansion should feel like one enormous interconnected location rather than a collection of unrelated levels.

The player should gradually learn its layout.

Areas can eventually connect through:

- Locked doors
- Secret passages
- Elevators
- Staircases
- Hidden rooms
- Museum exhibits
- Underground passages

Returning to previous areas should occasionally reveal new paths or secrets.

---

## Pillar 3 — The Museum Is Storytelling

Instead of explaining the entire history through exposition, the environment tells the story.

Examples:

- Paintings
- Statues
- Display cases
- Vampire artifacts
- Old journals
- Weapon collections
- Family portraits
- Museum descriptions
- Damaged exhibits

The player should slowly realize that the museum is hiding something.

---

## Pillar 4 — Gothic Atmosphere

The game should have a strong visual identity.

Key visual elements:

- Victorian architecture
- Stone halls
- Candlelight
- Moonlight
- Stained glass
- Dust
- Fog
- Old paintings
- Ornate furniture
- Crypts
- Hidden laboratories
- Ancient stone structures beneath the mansion

The atmosphere should be creepy without making the player helpless.

---

# 3. PLAYER CHARACTER

The player is a professional vampire hunter.

The character should initially have limited supernatural abilities but gradually acquire relics and powers from defeated vampires.

The player therefore becomes stronger throughout the game without simply receiving bigger numbers.

---

# 4. COMBAT SYSTEM

## Primary Weapon — Hunter's Revolver

A reliable ranged weapon.

Characteristics:

- Fast firing
- Accurate
- Limited ammunition
- Useful for interrupting enemies
- Can be upgraded

Potential special ammunition:

- Silver
- Holy
- Fire
- Armor-piercing

---

## Secondary Weapon — Relic Shotgun

A powerful close-range weapon.

Useful for:

- Staggering enemies
- Destroying groups
- Interrupting stronger attacks
- Creating space

---

## Melee — Fists

Bare-handed punches with the off hand. No sword.

The melee system should eventually support:

- Light attacks
- Heavy attacks
- Air attacks
- Launchers
- Enemy knockback
- Combos
- Finishers

The first prototype should remain simple.

Start with:

**Light attack + heavy attack + dodge.**

Expand only after the basic combat feels good.

---

# 5. MOVEMENT

Movement should be fast and responsive.

Basic abilities:

- Walk
- Run
- Jump
- Crouch
- Dodge/dash

## Perfect Dodge

A major combat mechanic.

If the player dodges at the correct moment:

- The attack misses
- A brief slow-motion effect occurs
- The player receives an opportunity to counterattack

This creates a skill-based combat loop without requiring extremely complicated mechanics.

---

# 6. STYLE SYSTEM

The game can track combat performance using a style meter.

Possible ranks:

**D — Dreadful**  
**C — Capable**  
**B — Brutal**  
**A — Astonishing**  
**S — Stylish**  
**SS — Supernatural**  
**SSS — Spectacular**

The meter increases when the player:

- Changes weapons
- Performs combos
- Gets headshots
- Uses melee attacks
- Performs perfect dodges
- Kills multiple enemies
- Uses environmental hazards

The meter decreases when the player:

- Takes damage
- Repeats the same attack excessively
- Avoids combat for too long

The style system should be optional for progression. It exists primarily to reward skilled players.

---

# 7. ENEMIES

## Vampire Thrall

Basic melee enemy.

Fast and aggressive.

Purpose: teaches basic combat.

---

## Gargoyle

Flying enemy.

Attacks from above and forces the player to look vertically.

---

## Blood Knight

Large armored enemy.

Slow but powerful.

Requires dodging and positioning.

---

## Vampire Bat Swarm

Small flying enemies.

Weak individually but dangerous in groups.

---

## Animated Museum Guardian

Statues or museum displays that come alive.

These enemies reinforce the museum concept.

---

## Noble Vampires

Major enemies and bosses.

Each noble vampire should have:

- A unique personality
- Unique visual design
- Unique combat mechanics
- A distinct museum exhibit
- A connection to the mansion's history

---

# 8. THE MUSEUM

The mansion should be divided into themed areas.

## The Grand Gallery

The main entrance.

Features:

- Huge staircase
- Giant chandelier
- Portraits
- Statues
- Museum exhibits

This becomes an important navigation hub.

---

## Vampire History Wing

Displays:

- Coffins
- Ancient weapons
- Vampire manuscripts
- Blood-related artifacts
- Family records

This is where the player begins realizing that the exhibits may be genuine.

---

## Natural History Wing

Contains:

- Taxidermied animals
- Giant bats
- Wolves
- Strange specimens

Some eventually become enemies.

---

## Royal Gallery

Contains portraits and statues of the vampire bloodline.

The player discovers that the family once ruled the surrounding region.

---

## The Archive

A library and research section.

Contains:

- Books
- Maps
- Journals
- Museum records
- Hidden documents

This area provides much of the game's lore.

---

## Underground Crypt

The architecture changes dramatically.

Victorian decoration disappears.

The player discovers an ancient structure beneath the mansion.

This area begins revealing the museum's true purpose.

---

# 9. BOSSES

Boss encounters should be major spectacles.

## Example Boss — The Curator

The museum's curator initially appears to be an ordinary human.

Throughout the game, the player encounters him.

Eventually he reveals himself as an ancient vampire.

The boss battle occurs in the Grand Gallery.

The environment becomes part of the fight:

- Statues attack
- Paintings fall
- Coffins open
- The boss moves between balconies
- Museum displays become hazards

The battle should test the player's complete combat toolkit.

---

# 10. STORY STRUCTURE

The player enters the mansion to investigate mysterious disappearances.

At first, the mansion appears abandoned.

The player discovers that the museum was built by an ancient vampire family.

Each major area reveals another piece of the family's history.

Eventually, the player discovers:

**The museum was never created to preserve history.**

It was created to contain something.

The vampire family has been maintaining the museum as a supernatural prison.

As the player defeats the vampires and destroys their relics, the prison begins to weaken.

The player's mission changes from:

**"Kill the vampires."**

to:

**"Find out what the vampires were keeping imprisoned."**

---

# 11. GAMEPLAY LOOP

The core gameplay loop is:

**Explore**
↓
**Discover an exhibit / secret**
↓
**Fight enemies**
↓
**Find resources or a key item**
↓
**Unlock another part of the mansion**
↓
**Learn more about the museum**
↓
**Fight a major enemy**
↓
**Gain a new ability or weapon**
↓
**Return to previous areas**
↓
**Discover new paths**
↓
**Progress deeper into the mansion**

---

# 12. VERTICAL SLICE

Before attempting the entire game, development should begin with a small playable demo.

The vertical slice should contain:

### One Area
The Grand Gallery and a connected corridor.

### One Basic Enemy
Vampire Thrall.

### One Weapon
Revolver.

### One Melee Attack
Punches (jab and heavy haymaker).

### One Movement Ability
Dodge.

### One Special Mechanic
Perfect Dodge.

### One Mini-Boss
A stronger vampire enemy.

### One Environmental Puzzle
A locked museum exhibit requiring exploration.

### One Story Reveal
The player discovers that the museum exhibits are real.

The goal is to determine whether the **movement + shooting + melee + atmosphere** are fun before building the rest of the game.

- Lighting
- Doors
- Keys

## Phase 5 — Full Production

# 14. UNITY TECHNOLOGY

Potential systems:

- Unity Character Controller
- Input System
- Animator
- NavMesh for enemies
- ScriptableObjects for weapons/enemies
- Particle systems/VFX
- Unity lighting
- Audio Mixer
- Cinemachine if appropriate
- Scene management
- Save/load system

The project should favor **simple, modular systems** over complicated architecture.

---

# 15. IMPORTANT SCOPE RULE

The game should NOT attempt to become a massive AAA-style project.

The initial target is:

**One excellent mansion.  
A small number of memorable enemies.  
A handful of weapons.  
Strong combat.  
Strong atmosphere.  
A compelling mystery.**

A polished 30–60 minute experience is preferable to an unfinished 10-hour game.

If the prototype becomes successful, the project can expand.

---

# 16. DESIGN GOAL

The ideal player reaction is:

> "I want to see what's in the next room."

followed by:

> "I want to fight that thing."

and eventually:

> "Wait... what is this museum actually hiding?"

The game should constantly alternate between **curiosity, exploration, and stylish action.**
